Renting in Hamburg

A one-bedroom flat in Hamburg costs about $1,900 a month. Landlords usually ask for a deposit of 2 months of rent, and the average commute to the centre is 31 minutes.

How the search works

Hamburg splits into a harbour side and an Alster side, and prices follow the water. The closer a street is to either, the less it matters what the flat itself looks like. Away from both, the city is a patchwork of quiet residential quarters that seldom appear in guides and often hold the best value.

Cooperative housing is a real option here. The waiting lists are long, but membership costs little, the rents stay low for decades and the cooperative does not sell the building from under you. Apply to several at once and expect to wait; in the meantime rent on the open market like everyone else.

Winterhude and Eimsbüttel are the usual targets, with old buildings, high ceilings and prices to match. Wilhelmsburg is cheaper and one stop from the centre, on an island in the river that the city has been building up for years. Barmbek and Hamm sit between the two in price and in character.

Ask about the flood line if the flat is near the Elbe, and about the cellar in particular. Ask too who clears the stairwell: in many older houses the tenants still take turns by a rota pinned to the door, and a landlord will mention it only after the first complaint from a neighbour.
